# Stage 2: shortlist on the work

The work is the only thing that matters when shortlisting. It's the best signal you can get for whether someone is worth talking to, precisely because, if done well, it can't be one-shotted.

If you're still shortlisting based on company logos and tenure, it's time to update that. There is *some* signal from knowing that someone worked at a big tech company for 10 years, but is it the signal that's useful for *your* startup?

**What it's for:** shortlisting isn't filtering a list. It's deciding, quickly and fairly, whose work you want to talk about.

## Method

### 1. Look at the work first

For each candidate, open the portfolio or the work they sent **before** the CV, the logos or the years. If you can browse, open the links yourself. If you can't, ask the hiring manager for the case studies or screenshots.

Judge the work against the role brief, not against taste:

- **The spike:** do you see the one or two things the role needs, done exceptionally? This is the main question.
- **Their part:** can you tell what they did and what the team did?
- **Judgement:** do they show the decisions and the trade-offs, or only the final screens?
- **Real:** is there work that shipped, at the level of craft this role needs?

Give each candidate **yes, talk**, **maybe** or **no**, with one line of evidence that points at a specific piece of work. "Strong visual craft" isn't evidence. "The onboarding redesign in the second case study, especially the empty states" is.

If the hiring manager has been screening on logos and tenure, redo the last pile with them this way and compare who comes out.

### 2. Keep it a first pass

This stage decides who's worth a conversation. The interviews and the work trial are where you go deep.

### 3. Make yourself spearfish-able

Some designers get hired by spearfishing: they take screenshots of a company's product, look for a problem in the interface, make a small change and send it to the hiring manager. Make it easy for designers to play with your product:

- Can someone use your product without a sales call? A free tier, a demo account or a sandbox.
- Is there a way to send work to the hiring manager that a real person reads?

Check both with the hiring manager and write down what's missing.

## Borrowed from orchestras: work first, names after

Orchestras started auditioning players behind a screen, so that the jury heard the playing without knowing who it was. Borrow the order, if not the screen: look at the work first, the names and logos after. A strict version: someone strips names and employers from the case studies before the first pass.

## If the pile is thin

No one with the spike? Say so. See "When the shortlist is thin" in `SKILL.md`.

## Checks

1. Calibration: before the hiring manager does the whole pile, they do a handful and you do the same ones. Compare. Where you disagree, check whether the spike in the brief is clear.
2. The cold read in `judges.md` ("Shortlist"). This one has candidates' details in it: run it with a fresh model, never by sending it to someone outside the company.
