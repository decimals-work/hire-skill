# Stage 4: set up a work trial

This is the most valuable part of the whole process.

And not just because it's your opportunity to assess the work. It's your opportunity to share what this person is signing up for: pros and cons.

Avoid obsessing too much over the creation of an artefact. If all you're assessing is how someone takes a written task and delivers an output, you're missing the point. They're not a language model. Think of hiring as a contact sport: don't avoid friction.

**What it's for:** a work trial isn't a test. It's a short rehearsal of working together, with a real problem, the real people and a real disagreement in it.

## Method

### 1. Pick a real problem near the spike

A real problem from the product, close to the spike in the role brief, scoped so real progress fits in the time.

### 2. Pay for it

Pay for at least a full day's worth of work trial. We recommend at least $1,000 per day (or the equivalent in your currency). Put the amount, and when it's paid, in writing before the day.

### 3. Work together, not at a distance

The hiring manager works *with* the candidate during the trial, not just receives a deliverable at the end. Plan:

- **The kick-off:** the problem, what's known, what good looks like.
- **A check-in:** give a real steer, and see what they do with it.
- **A scenario where the answer isn't straightforward:** you want to see how someone navigates uncertainty and disagreement. That's the job.
- **The wrap-up:** they show where they got to and why. You tell them honestly what working with you is like, including the cons.

### 4. Settle the practical questions

Whether AI tools are allowed. What they can use from your product, data and design system. Confidentiality, and any data they mustn't see. Who owns the work (the hiring manager decides this, with legal advice if they need it). Travel, if it's in person. Sending any materials the day before.

Fill in `templates/work-trial-brief.md`.

## Borrowed from engineering: pairing

Engineers pair with candidates to see the thinking, not just the result. Run the trial the same way: how they think and how they take a steer matter as much as what they make.

## Checks

1. The brief covers: the real problem, the pay and when it's paid, the time, who they'll work with, the scenario with no straightforward answer, and the practical questions in step 4.
2. The cold read in `judges.md` ("Work trial").

## Hand over

`hire/work-trial.md`: the filled-in brief, the day's schedule, and what each person working with the candidate is looking for.
